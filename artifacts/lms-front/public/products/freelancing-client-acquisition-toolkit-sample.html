Sample 01

Choose a clear service before outreach

Potential clients respond more easily when they can understand what you do, who it is for and what business problem it addresses. Replace a broad label such as “I do digital marketing” with a clear service statement.

Service statement template

I help [specific type of customer] improve [specific business outcome] by providing [defined service or deliverable].

Qualification checklist

  • The prospect appears to use or need the type of service you provide.
  • You can identify a relevant observation without inventing a problem.
  • The organisation has a legitimate public presence and contact route.
  • Your message can explain why you selected this prospect specifically.
  • You can deliver the work responsibly if the prospect accepts.
Responsible outreach: personalise messages, respect opt-outs, avoid scraping private data and do not send misleading mass claims.

Sample 02

A professional first message

The goal of the first message is not to force a sale. It is to establish relevance, show that you reviewed the prospect’s situation and invite a low-pressure next step.

Outreach structure

Subject: A specific idea for [business/project]

Hello [name],

I noticed [truthful, relevant observation]. I work with [relevant audience] on [defined service]. One possible improvement could be [concise idea], because [reason connected to a business outcome].

If this is currently a priority, I can send a short outline of the approach and likely scope. If not, no problem.

Regards,
[name and portfolio/contact]

Before sending

  • Verify the person’s name and role.
  • Remove exaggerated claims and guaranteed outcomes.
  • Keep the message readable on a phone.
  • Include one clear next step, not several competing requests.

Sample 03

Discovery call question bank

A discovery call should help both sides decide whether the project is a suitable fit. Ask only questions that help clarify the work.

Goals and context

  • What prompted you to consider this project now?
  • What should be different when the project is complete?
  • Who will use, review or approve the deliverable?

Scope and constraints

  • What already exists, and what needs to be created?
  • Are there fixed technical, brand, legal or timeline constraints?
  • What content, access or decisions will your team provide?

Decision and next steps

  • Who makes the final decision?
  • What timeline is realistic for reviewing a proposal?
  • Is there a budget range approved for this work?
Do not promise a price or deadline until you understand dependencies, revision expectations and approval stages.

Sample 04

Proposal essentials

  1. Context: a concise summary of the client’s stated problem and goal.
  2. Scope: precise deliverables and explicit exclusions.
  3. Process: milestones, feedback stages and responsibilities.
  4. Timeline: estimated dates dependent on timely inputs and approvals.
  5. Commercial terms: price, taxes if applicable, payment milestones and validity period.
  6. Change control: how work outside scope will be estimated and approved.
  7. Next step: how the client can accept and begin onboarding.
